journal: "Submit to the *Review of Accouting Studies*"
abstract: "This paper examines how firms navigate political uncertainty through the political tone of their corporate disclosures. Using U.S. gubernatorial elections as plausibly exogenous shocks to state-level political uncertainty, the analysis shows that firms shift toward a more politically neutral (“bipartisan”) disclosure tone in pre‑election periods, with stronger effects for firms in politically sensitive, highly visible, and domestically oriented industries. Politically neutral language is associated with lower stock return volatility during election quarters and mitigates post‑election sales declines for firms that had financially supported the losing party, consistent with neutral tone functioning as a political risk‑management tool. Examining campaign contributions, the paper further documents that firms often combine partisan political giving with more neutral disclosure tone when uncertainty is high, indicating that managers use disclosure to hedge, rather than mirror, their underlying political alignments."
